How this world works

Money by fiat. Nobody has a budget in their own currency. Paying a wage or buying something creates new units of the payer's currency. When a business is paid in its owner's currency, those units are retired.

The exchange. Each currency has a pool that trades it against X. X has a fixed supply. To buy from someone, a shopper sells their own money for X and buys the seller's money with it, so printing pushes a currency down and customers pull it up.

Work and business. Everyone has different skill at making food, goods and services. Businesses set prices and wages in their owner's currency, hire when they keep selling out and lay off when stock piles up. Workers take the job whose pay converts to the most X, so a sliding currency loses its staff.

Bonds. An issuer sells bonds for its own currency, which takes that money out of circulation today. At maturity, 30 ticks later, it prints the principal plus interest. Buyers demand a yield that beats the currency's recent slide; issuers stop selling above 60% per term.

Policy. Each central bank sits somewhere between hawk and dove. Doves print more and tolerate a faster slide. When a currency falls faster than its issuer tolerates, the issuer stops printing, sells more bonds and spends X reserves to buy its money back. When it rises too fast, the issuer prints more.

How they feel. Each entity has six feelings: being fed, comfort, security (X reserves and having work), purpose (running a business beats a job beats idleness), pride in its currency, and standing next to everyone else. Mood is that level plus how it compares with what the entity has grown used to, so a fall hurts more than steady hardship and good times fade into normal. Diaries record what happens to them in their own words.

Save files. Save run writes either a full save, which holds the whole history and the exact state so the run can be loaded and continued, or a short summary for pasting into a chat. Every settings change is logged with the tick it happened.

Redenomination. A currency that falls below a millionth of an X is replaced at 1,000,000 old for 1 new. Its value history carries on unbroken.
